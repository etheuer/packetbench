# Packetbench conversion template

One template. Twenty desks. Dedicated landing pages, not a catalog.

Sources used to lock the requirements (2026 B2B landing-page practice):

- Problem-first hero, not brand-first
- Headline states the buyer problem; subhead states the mechanism
- One goal per page; one CTA label; CTA visible without scroll
- Repeat that same CTA at the form
- Strip navigation that sends people away
- Short form (only what we need to start)
- Proof next to the form
- Fast, readable, mobile-first

We have no customers yet. Fake logos, fake quotes, and fake metrics are forbidden.
Proof on this template is the packet itself, the named public trigger, the
turnaround, the price, and the risk boundary (you keep the login).

## Required sections (in this order)

1. **Header.** Wordmark only. No Desks / Terms in the first viewport.
2. **Hero.** Problem headline (max 8 words). Mechanism subhead (max 20 words).
   Price and turnaround. Primary CTA. Packet preview as the visual.
3. **Fit.** Who this is for. Who should not buy.
4. **Process.** Three named actions (verb + noun). No "Step 1".
5. **Offer.** Price, what is in, what we will not do.
6. **Objections.** Three real questions (login, outcome, time).
7. **Request.** Same CTA. Form fields: name, work email, what is due.
   Beside the form: process proof, not testimonials.
8. **Footer.** Legal limit in plain language. Terms and privacy.

## Hard rules

- One conversion goal: request the packet.
- CTA label is always `Request this packet`.
- No competing offers on the page.
- No invented results, clients, or ratings.
- No em-dashes.
- Form is 3 fields. Company is optional and off the required path.
- If intake is unwired, fail in plain language.

## Pass check before deploy

- Headline is the buyer's problem.
- CTA is visible in the first screen on a 390px phone and a 1280px desktop.
- Packet contents are specific to that desk.
- A stranger can answer: what is this, why now, what do I get, what does it
  cost, what happens next, what you will not do.
